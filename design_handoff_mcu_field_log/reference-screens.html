What you need to know going in

Peter Parker's identity as Spider-Man was broadcast to the world at the end of the last film, and this one opens on the fallout — he is still a high-school senior, and college applications, not superheroics, drive the early going. The full plot

With his identity publicly exposed and his loved ones' lives upended, Peter asks Doctor Strange to cast a spell making the world forget he's Spider-Man — but his own hesitations corrupt it, tearing open the multiverse and pulling in villains, and eventually other Spider-Men, from universes entirely their own. After a version of Aunt May is killed trying to save one of the villains, Peter chooses to redeem all of them rather than send them back to certain death, at the cost of having Strange erase the world's memory of him entirely.

Why it matters

A crossover that unites three generations of live-action Spider-Man actors, and an ending — nobody remembers Peter Parker — that fundamentally isolates him from the rest of the Avengers for future stories.

Why this order

Placed here as the direct continuation of Far From Home's ending, unfolding a few months later.

  • Multiversal visitors: confirms the existence of alternate universes, directly enabling the next Doctor Strange film. Spoils: Multiverse of Madness
  • The world forgetting Peter Parker: resets Peter's status quo, isolating him from the other Avengers for future stories.
